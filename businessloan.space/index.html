Questions owners ask first

How long does it take to apply?

My application takes about 15 seconds to start. You’ll answer basic questions about your business there — that’s where your personal and business details go, on my secure application page, not on businessloan.space.

What are the basic requirements?

At least 4 months in business and $10,000 or more in monthly gross sales. Final eligibility, amounts, and terms are determined after review of your application — they aren’t guaranteed by answering the 4 questions on this page.

Do I need perfect credit to get business funding?

No. Myth: you need perfect credit to get business funding. Truth: no minimum FICO to apply — most top options start at 660. Check in minutes: businessloan.space. Bad credit does not automatically block an application, though credit can affect the amount and terms offered.

What if I make less than $10,000 a month?

You are not automatically out. $10,000+ a month and 4+ months in business are the fastest route to the top working capital options, not the only route — other options, such as equipment financing and startup funding, have different criteria. Check the options and review any offer before you accept it.

How fast could I get funded?

Same-day funding is a possibility for qualified businesses.* Timing depends on your application, verification, and the product you choose. Don’t count on a specific timeline until you’ve reviewed an actual offer.

Is Paul Webb a lender?

No. I’m Paul Webb — businessloan.space is my page to help you check if you qualify. When you tap “Continue to My Application,” you go straight to my application to finish. I’m not a lender or bank and I don’t make credit decisions.

What does it cost to check?

There’s no fee charged on this page to answer the questions or to click through and check. If you’re offered financing, review all costs, rates, repayment terms, and total repayment amount on the offer you receive before you accept anything.

What types of businesses use this?

Contractors, restaurants and food businesses, retail shops, service companies, trucking, and other established small businesses that have a clear use for the funds — equipment, inventory, expansion, working capital, and similar business needs.
